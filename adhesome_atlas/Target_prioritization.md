# Prioritization of Schistosome Integrin–Adhesome Candidates for Further Investigation

Based on the current dataset, the most promising candidates fall into three categories: tegument-associated vaccine or antibody targets, functionally important proteins for therapeutic investigation, and divergent protein families for evolutionary and mechanistic characterization. Our proposed initial candidates are tetraspanins, calpains, and integrin receptors. These combine high-confidence family annotation with either predicted membrane association, comparative conservation, or previously published functional evidence. For a manageable first experimental phase, the following candidate sets are recommended:

**Library-based prioritization context.** The current workbook records 29 grade-A tetraspanin members in OG0000303, distributed across all seven *Schistosoma* species, and six grade-A members each in OG0010672 and OG0011637. Sm-p80 and SmCalp2 are grade-A calpain-family candidates in separate orthogroups (OG0001071 and OG0008492), both represented in all seven species. The integrin-β family is represented by one grade-A candidate per species in OG0000253; integrin-α candidates are distributed across OG0000076, OG0000719 and OG0006637. This conservation and molecular annotation support selecting conserved representatives alongside sequence-divergent candidates.


1. **Recommended priority candidates**

## **Target 1: Tetraspanin-like proteins** 

- 159 supported candidates across 13 orthogroups
- Key orthogroups: OG0000303, OG0010672, OG0011637
- Representative S. mansoni sequences: Smp_059530, Smp_154180, Smp_142420, Smp_174190

The library assigns all four representative sequences to **grade A, specific-supported tetraspanin-like families**. Smp_059530 and Smp_154180 belong to OG0000303 and each has four predicted transmembrane helices, a cell-membrane localization prediction and a CDD tetraspanin large-extracellular-loop (LEL/EC2) annotation. Smp_142420 maps to OG0010672 with four predicted transmembrane helices and a membrane-localization prediction. Smp_174190 maps to OG0011637 and also carries a tetraspanin-domain assignment and membrane-related localization prediction. Accordingly, EC2 sequence alignment, predicted surface accessibility and conservation across species can be undertaken immediately using the annotated sequences. **These are family-supported atlas candidates; published Sm-TSP-2 vaccine evidence establishes the antigen-class rationale, rather than a one-to-one identity for every listed sequence.**

Published work provides a strong precedent: Sm-TSP-2 has undergone clinical evaluation as a vaccine antigen, and a 2025 mRNA vaccine study reported reduced worm and egg burdens in vaccinated mice ([R08 · Tran et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1430), [R10 · de Oliveira et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41541-025-01301-y)). The listed orthogroups are tetraspanin-family candidates for antigen screening.

The literature adds several complementary selection criteria. Recombinant Sm-TSP-2 elicited protection against worm and egg burdens in challenge experiments, and TSP-2 was recognized by antibodies from naturally resistant individuals ([Tran et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm1430)). Suppression of Sm-tsp-1/2 altered tegument organization and reduced parasite recovery, linking this antigen class to surface integrity as well as vaccine accessibility ([Tran et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1000840)). A 2025 Sm-TSP-2 mRNA vaccine study reported that membrane-anchored EC2 constructs reduced worm and egg burdens by 66.7% and 66.9%, respectively, in a mouse model, supporting an **EC2-presentation-led screening strategy** ([Oliveira et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41541-025-01301-y)). Structural investigation of Sm-TSP-2-EC2 and its associated membrane microdomains further supports examining extracellular-loop conformation and membrane-protein partners ([Sm-TSP-2 structural study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24429291/) and [R09 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M113.531558)). For OG0000303, prioritize accessible EC2 segments shared among the 29 supported members; for OG0010672 and OG0011637, compare EC2 diversity and select representatives with distinctive extracellular motifs.


**Expanded tetraspanin selection from the same library.** Besides the three initially highlighted orthogroups, OG0001078 contains **28 specific-supported** tetraspanin-like proteins and OG0000534 contains **19**, making both useful for expanding the comparative EC2 screening panel beyond OG0000303 (29 members). Compare diagnostic four-transmembrane architectures, EC2-loop sequences, predicted membrane placement and the range of sequences present among the seven *Schistosoma* species. Prioritize representative antigen constructs by loop accessibility and within/between-species conservation rather than treating every tetraspanin as an established Sm-TSP-2 equivalent. This selection follows the experimentally supported importance of schistosome surface and subtegumental proteins for antigen discovery ([tegument proteomics study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpara.2015.03.004)).


## **Target 2 — Calpain-like cysteine proteases** 

- 41 supported candidates across four orthogroups
- Key orthogroups: OG0001071, OG0008492

These orthogroups includes two relevant S. mansoni proteins:
- Smp_214190 (SmCalp1/Sm-p80): experimentally characterized tegument-associated calpain and an established vaccine-research antigen (Molehin, McManus and You, 2022)[https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms23042255].
- Smp_137410 (SmCalp2): a second characterized tegumental calpain, suitable for comparative functional analysis (Wang et al., 2017)[https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-13141-5].

The workbook independently supports **41 grade-A calpain-like candidates in four orthogroups**. Smp_214190 belongs to OG0001071 (13 supported proteins across seven species), whereas Smp_137410 belongs to OG0008492 (seven supported proteins, one per species). Both have recognizable calpain protease-core and C-terminal EF-hand-related architectures. This family offers a particularly strong bridge between bioinformatic discovery and translational testing: living schistosomes display extracellular, calcium-dependent calpain activity, and tegumental calpains are associated with cleavage of host fibronectin ([Wang et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-13141-5)). SmCalp2 also differs from classical calpains at a catalytic histidine-equivalent site (glutamine substitution), making comparative substrate profiling and catalytic-site characterization especially informative. For Sm-p80, a published Phase 1 human study reported acceptable safety and induced anti-Sm-p80 IgG responses, establishing this candidate as a useful antigen-development reference ([Jackson et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41541-025-01261-3)). A practical next phase is to compare calpain-core conservation, substrate cleavage, antibody binding and cross-species antigen conservation for OG0001071 and OG0008492.


**Expanded calpain selection from the same library.** OG0000148 contains **14 specific-supported** calpain-like proteins, while OG0006377 contains **seven**. Both can complement the existing Sm-p80/SmCalp2 reference set by providing additional protease-family comparators for domain-completeness assessment, catalytic-residue inspection and calcium-dependent activity screening. The existing literature on tegument-associated calpain activity offers an assay framework, but localization and substrate preferences should be assessed for the selected protein itself ([Wang et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-13141-5)).


## **Target 3 — Integrin α/β receptors**

- 17 supported integrin α, seven supported integrin β
- Key orthogroups: OG0000253, OG0000076, OG0000719, OG0006637
- Two S. mansoni candidates deserve particular consideration:

Protein ID	Assignment	Main justification
Smp_089700	Supported integrin β	Previously characterized Smβ-Int1
Smp_126140	Supported integrin α	Published Smα-Int1 locus and supporting family architecture

Smβ-Int1 RNA interference produced oocyte abnormalities. The published Smα-Int2 cDNA maps to Smp_170280 in OG0006637. The current audit records this candidate as **Integrin α-like (Ig3 segment), unresolved, grade D**. Reconcile the current sequence model with the cloned transcript before receptor-pairing and ligand assays ([R01 · Beckmann et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0052519)).

The current library provides a coherent receptor-focused starting set: **OG0000253 includes seven specific-supported integrin-β candidates, one in each species**, and OG0000076 and OG0000719 each contain seven specific-supported integrin-α candidates. Smp_089700 contains annotated integrin-β and cytoplasmic-tail domains with a predicted transmembrane helix; Smp_126140 has supported integrin-α architecture and a predicted C-terminal transmembrane helix. Their prioritization is reinforced by published cloning and RNA-interference work linking Smβ-Int1 to schistosome oogenesis and reproductive kinase interactions ([Beckmann et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0052519)). Subsequent work provides direct biological context for a **Smβ-Int1–ILK–PINCH–Nck2–SmVKR1** signalling hypothesis in the ovary, including co-immunoprecipitation evidence and ILK perturbation phenotypes affecting oocyte survival and egg production ([Gelmedin et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1006147)). These results support testing integrin pairing, gonadal expression, receptor-associated adaptor recruitment and fecundity-related phenotypes.

**Evidence supporting additional priorities.** The Src-family category comprises **30 specific-supported candidates in two orthogroups**, including Smp_006920 and Smp_136300 in OG0000122; the current CDD records retain their SH3, SH2 and catalytic protein-kinase features. Schistosome Src kinase SmTK3 has been localized to reproductive tissues, shown to possess tyrosine kinase activity and associated with cytoskeletal-binding partners ([Kapp et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molbiopara.2004.07.010)). The SmTK3/SmTK6–SmTK4 signalling work provides additional rationale for prioritizing kinase-mediated gonadal processes ([SmTK4 study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1000769)). This makes kinase activity, substrate phosphorylation and reproductive phenotypes suitable first-line functional assays.

The atlas also contains **six specific-supported FAK candidates** (OG0001975), **eight ILK candidates** (OG0003979), **14 kindlin candidates** (OG0000311), **14 specific-supported Talin-like candidates** (OG0001037) and **14 vinculin candidates** (OG0000580 and OG0003686). The representative Smp_053420 kindlin contains a kindlin-type FERM-F0 domain and PH domain; Smp_167010 talin has FERM-related and I/LWEQ features; and Smp_079760 ILK carries ankyrin-repeat architecture. In metazoan adhesion systems, talin and kindlin bind integrin cytoplasmic tails, while talin–vinculin coupling connects receptors to actin and provides force-responsive scaffolding ([R16 · del Rio et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1162912), [Calderwood et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrm3624); [integrin adhesion review](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.190991)). The previously characterized schistosome ILK/PINCH/VKR1 pathway gives an especially direct reason to prioritize this set for candidate co-expression, protein-complex and signalling assays ([Gelmedin et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1006147)).

The **75 broad-supported collagen-like, 35 broad-supported laminin-like and seven specific-supported Laminin-like candidates** can be used as an extracellular-interface discovery set. In the library, Smp_135560 carries collagen-repeat CDD annotations and a predicted secretion signal, while Smp_146840 has laminin-EGF-related domains and an extracellular localization prediction. They are suitable for evaluating parasite extracellular matrix organization and potential ligands or attachment environments alongside receptor-focused studies. These data motivate sequence-domain comparisons and parasite-versus-host recognition assays as positive selection steps.

**Supported receptor-proximal scaffold priorities.** The current audit assigns **14 specific-supported `Parvin_like` candidates to OG0000818**; these include Smp_171530 (two CH domains, cytoplasmic/membrane-compatible DeepLoc prediction) and Smp_310240 (parvin-specific CH CDD profiles, cytoplasmic prediction). The dataset also contains **14 PINCH-like candidates** in OG0009156 and OG0009923, each represented across all seven species, and **seven paxillin-like candidates** in OG0001153. Smp_020540 and Smp_165070 show five-repeat LIM architectures, while Smp_315700 carries a four-LIM architecture. These families are immediate additions because the **ILK–PINCH–parvin (IPP)** module links integrin signalling to the actin cytoskeleton in metazoan adhesions ([R17 · Legate et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrm1789), [R18 · Zhu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.4014), [R19 · Huet-Calderwood et al.](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.155879)). In *S. mansoni*, Gelmedin et al. cloned SmILK (Smp_079760), SmPINCH (Smp_020540.2) and SmNck2 (Smp_014850) for the Smβ-Int1–SmVKR1 study. The parvin and Smp_165070 candidate records carry pathway context. A discriminating first experiment is to compare the ILK–PINCH–parvin complex hypothesis with ILK–PINCH–Nck adaptor associations using gonadal expression, co-immunoprecipitation, actin-binding assays and perturbation of oocyte organization ([Gelmedin et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1006147); [Ain et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00418-025-02408-y); [integrin adhesion organization review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892292/)).

**Adaptor-to-actin signalling expansion.** The atlas records **seven specific-supported Nck candidates in OG0002127** (Smp_014850 has multiple SH3 domains and a terminal SH2 domain), **nine specific-supported PAK-like candidates** in OG0009134/OG0000497 (Smp_064040 and Smp_347600 have PBD/CRIB- and kinase-associated CDD profiles), and **25 supported Rho GTPase-like candidates** distributed across OG0000876, OG0009113 and OG0008033. Together they define a targeted receptor-adaptor–small-GTPase–kinase–actin test set. Prioritize GTP-loaded Rho-family binding, PAK activation and actin-network morphology; use the published schistosome integrin–VKR association as the species-specific pathway starting point, and the established metazoan integrin-adhesion network as the mechanistic basis for selecting these families ([Gelmedin et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1006147); [integrin-adhesion review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892292/)).

**Kinase and lipid-signalling opportunities.** The workbook contains **20 supported PKC-like candidates** in OG0006660 and OG0002375. Smp_128480 has tandem C1, C2 and catalytic kinase features and is independently represented in published schistosome phosphoproteomic/kinomic work. Schistosome PKC inhibition or activation has been associated with changes in movement, attachment, pairing and egg release, providing unusually direct phenotypic selection criteria for this additional family ([PKC/ERK functional study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24921927/); [phosphoproteomic analysis](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0008115)). For local membrane-proximal signalling, **14 PIP5K-like candidates** (Smp_315710 in OG0000988 and Smp_328600 in OG0000789) provide a complementary phosphoinositide-regulation track; test lipid-kinase activity and dependence of recruitment of kindlin/talin-associated components rather than assuming a specific lipid substrate from the family label alone.

**Cytoskeletal and surface-associated candidates.** The library additionally assigns **14 alpha-actinin** proteins (OG0008512/OG0009147), **21 filamin** proteins (OG0000801/OG0008499) and **14 ARPC2-like** proteins (OG0002862). In *S. mansoni*, Smp_014780 and Smp_034550 contain paired CH domains and spectrin repeats; Smp_000100 and Smp_317420 carry CH plus extensive filamin-repeat architectures; and Smp_010690 and Smp_127830 contain the Arp2/3 P34-Arc domain. These should be tested as actin-bundling, cortical organization and branching contributors within the candidate adhesome ([integrin-adhesion organization review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892292/)). On the host-facing membrane, **seven specific-supported ADAM-like metalloproteases** across OG0000056 and OG0001152 include Smp_313450 with a predicted secretion signal, transmembrane segment, metalloprotease and disintegrin domains; prioritize biochemical protease assays and surface exposure measurements. Existing tegument proteomics establishes an experimentally practical setting for testing surface-associated parasite proteins ([schistosomula tegument proteomics](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpara.2015.03.004)).

**Exploratory channel and divergent-family candidates.** The atlas includes **37 specific-supported KCNH-like channels** in three orthogroups (OG0000881, OG0000629 and OG0000974); representative Smp_148670 and Smp_152350 have PAS, ion-transporter and cyclic-nucleotide-binding-associated domains, multiple predicted membrane-spanning helices and membrane localization. Potassium channels are a recognized part of the schistosome ion-channel repertoire, warranting electrophysiological and comparative pharmacological profiling; **these KCNH candidates are not the TRPM channel associated with praziquantel action** ([*S. mansoni* genome study](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08160); [native praziquantel-activated current study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39816816/)). The selected `Audited_family_assignment!B50` sequence, `Sbovis__SBOV2_16320`, belongs to **OG0001016**, with seven broad-supported protein kinase superfamily candidates with unresolved subtype across all seven schistosome species, including Smp_176580. Its SH3–protein kinase–PBD Pfam organization and mixed Stage 1 Src/Abl hypotheses make it a strong **targeted family-rule refinement** case. Run competing family-specific CDD/HMM profiles and examine domain order and catalytic integrity before assigning a Src, Abl or PAK family name. The current support is broad (grade C) at protein kinase superfamily level.


3. **A practical target-selection framework**

Evaluation criterion	Vaccine/antibody candidate	Small-molecule drug candidate
Family identification	Supported identity, preferably with characterized homologues	Supported identity and defined molecular function
Accessibility	Exposed tegumental or secreted region demonstrated experimentally	Target must be accessible to the compound
Biological importance	Contribution to parasite establishment, survival, fecundity or transmission	Strong loss-of-function phenotype
Selectivity	Low risk of antibody cross-reactivity with host proteins	Parasite-selective binding or inhibition
Conservation	Conserved target epitopes across intended species and parasite populations	Conserved targetable residues across intended species
Feasibility	Recombinant antigen, immune assays and challenge model	Biochemical assay, structural model and chemical starting points

For practical ranking, prioritize candidates by **convergence of evidence**: (1) specific-supported identity and relevant diagnostic architecture; (2) published schistosome functional, localization, immunological or interaction evidence; (3) cross-species orthogroup representation and suitable sequence conservation; (4) predicted epitope or biochemical accessibility; and (5) a concrete assay path that can discriminate competing biological hypotheses. On this basis, **Sm-p80 (Smp_214190) and tetraspanin EC2 representatives** provide the strongest vaccine/antibody development anchors; **Smβ-Int1 (Smp_089700) with ILK/PINCH-associated candidates** provides a priority reproductive signalling system; **SmCalp2 (Smp_137410)** supports comparative surface-protease characterization; and **Src/FAK/talin/kindlin/vinculin** provide a coherent set for testing adhesion-associated signal integration. The atlas family grades and localization/domain outputs can be used to select paired conserved and divergent proteins for each experimental objective. Literature-supported pathway roles are used as a rationale for selection; the library assignments specify which current sequences to investigate. **Expanded first-pass ranking:** (i) *receptor/scaffold*: parvin (OG0000818), PINCH (OG0009156/OG0009923), paxillin (OG0001153) and Nck (OG0002127); (ii) *signal transduction*: PKC (OG0006660/OG0002375), PAK (OG0009134), Rho-family GTPases (OG0008033) and PIP5K (OG0000789/OG0000988); (iii) *cytoskeletal and surface mechanism*: alpha-actinin, filamin, ARPC2 and ADAM-like proteins; and (iv) *discovery and refinement*: KCNH-like channels and the unresolved OG0001016 SH3–kinase–PBD group. This expands the tested molecular interfaces without changing the existing assay-selection criteria or the original family-support designations.
